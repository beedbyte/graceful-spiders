# Private source and scope check: growing prescribed depths

9 October 2026. Read-only literature comparison; this report is the only new research artifact from this subtask. No mathematical acceptance, publication permission or worldwide originality verdict is issued.

## Target and finding

The frozen [candidate proof](../graceful-whole-arm-next-2026-10-09/proof.md) asserts: for integers `s>=1`, `r>=3s`, `r!=3s+1`, `k=2r+1`, an alpha labeling of `P_(2k+1)` has threshold and midpoint label `k-1`, zero at arm depth `4s`, maximum `2k` at adjacent depth `4s+1`, and endpoints `(3k-1)/2,(3k+1)/2`. Known amalgamation then supplies two separate prescribed-zero spider labelings for every `n>=2,m>=0` in `S(k^n,1^m)`. Its convenient uniform subset has `2 floor((k-5)/6)` depths for every odd `k>=11`. This comparison does not assume that assertion has passed independent mathematical audit.

**Checked results do establish the ingredients, substantial boundary cases, and ordinary gracefulness of the target spiders. The checked statements do not supply the simultaneous midpoint-index and specified internal extreme pair. Their statements alone therefore do not subsume the asserted full two-parameter growing-depth consequence. Historical non-subsumption remains unresolved because Cattell's full construction and the original older alpha-path texts were not obtained.**

## What the exact prior statements cover

**Patterson (2017), full local primary thesis.** Re-read printed pp.10–11 and 15 in [the complete extraction](../source-papers/patterson-2017-thesis.txt), checked against the established PDF pagination. Theorem 3.1.3 records Rosa's alpha prescribed-zero path result, except the midpoint of `P5`. Theorem 3.1.2 gives alpha-index complementation. Theorem 3.2.1, printed p.11 / PDF p.12, proves zero-vertex amalgamation and explicitly moves the selected alpha vertex to its index. Lemma 3.5.1, printed p.15, records Cattell's endpoint alpha-label choice, excluding `n=4s+1`, labels `s,3s`. Corollary 2.4.3 gives uniform-spider center zero; Theorem 5.3.6 supplies reversible leaf-arm growth, and Conjecture 5.4.4 is still a conjecture. [Institutional original](https://cardinalscholar.bsu.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/b44ff232-a480-4d64-aa44-8dff4535e28c/content).

**Luiz–Campos–Richter (2017), original IC-17-12.** Lemma 4, printed p.3, gives a selected vertex zero under an alpha labeling with the `P5` exception. Lemma 5 records Cattell's arbitrary one-vertex graceful-label result under its stated conditions. Theorem 14, printed p.6 / PDF p.7, gives complete zero-rotatability for a path with a leaf identified at a central vertex. Thus `n=2,m=0` and `n=2,m=1` are already covered for every arm length. Lemma 13 describes contiguous alpha bipartition intervals, which fix the threshold but do not fix which vertex receives it. [Original report](https://ic.unicamp.br/~reltech/2017/17-12.pdf).

**Shan–Zhong (2026), v2 original.** Lemma 1 explicitly allows amalgamation at the alpha index or zero with a graceful zero vertex. Lemma 2(a–c) identifies Cattell Theorem 3 and Corollary 1 as sources for path zero, alpha zero, and endpoint alpha choice. Theorem 5 concerns ordinary gracefulness when all but three legs have length at most two. These are existential or one-vertex constraints; none of these statements has the candidate's simultaneous pair and midpoint requirement. [Original v2, Sections 1–2](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.14295v2).

**Cattell (2007), original publisher abstract only.** *Graceful labellings of paths*, Discrete Mathematics 307, 3161–3176, DOI 10.1016/j.disc.2007.03.046. The abstract concerns prescribing one arbitrary vertex label, characterizes when alpha labelings exist, and says its machinery uses pi-representations and earlier Kotzig/Rosa results. Fresh direct opening of the publisher full-text endpoint failed; search exposed the abstract, not the article. No exact two-vertex theorem number, proof comparison, or assertion of its absence can be justified from that evidence. The paper's scope is broader than endpoints. [Publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0012365X07001215).

## Why the single-vertex results alone are insufficient

This paragraph is our quantifier and transformation analysis, not a quotation or claim about unread original proofs. Set `q=2k`, `A=k-1`. For odd k, the midpoint belongs to the smaller bipartition of `P_(2k+1)`, of size k; if that side is low, its threshold is forced to A. That fixes a *number*, not the midpoint's individual label.

Rosa's theorem applied to a selected arm vertex gives an alpha labeling with that vertex zero. It supplies no midpoint label. Applied to the midpoint instead, it gives midpoint zero; alpha-index complementation sends that zero to A. It supplies no location for the resulting zero, because that location was the A-labeled vertex before complementation. These two applications may give different labelings. Combining their separate existence assertions is invalid.

For any graceful labeling, edge difference q forces zero and maximum to be adjacent. This does mean that a selected zero vertex automatically has a maximum at *some neighbor*. An internal path vertex has two neighbors, so the zero theorem does not choose which one carries the maximum; more critically it does not enforce midpoint A at the same time. In the candidate this neighbor is fixed as depth `4s+1`, and the midpoint is fixed simultaneously.

The known amalgamation shifts residual labels by A and high path labels by the residual edge count Q. It preserves path zero and changes its maximum to `Q+2k`; it requires the shared midpoint to carry A. If midpoint zero is used in the older theorem, index complementation makes it A and destroys zero there. Ordinary amalgamation therefore cannot preserve a chosen interior zero without already having the candidate's required compatible path labeling. This identifies the missing input precisely; it does not prove that older constructions cannot furnish it.

## Walecki, twizzler and symmetric doubling overlap

The candidate's residue-2 tail is exactly an affine Walecki alternating order, hence an existing ingredient. The residue-0/1 prefix is a zigzag `(x,y,x+3,y-3,...)`; the general zigzag shape is established terminology. Direct recognition of this whole residue-3 permutation as an older named construction was not achieved.

**Ollis–Willmott (2011), original publisher PDF.** *On twizzler, zigzag and graceful terraces*, Australasian Journal of Combinatorics 51, 243–257. Definition on p.244 gives zigzag subsequences. Theorem 1, p.245, gives graceful p-twizzlers when length is a multiple of p; Theorems 2–3 address imperfect twizzlers. Table 1, p.249, includes the length-6 start-1 sequence `1,5,0,3,2,4`, different from candidate `1,3,4,0,5,2`. Theorem 4, p.249, joins graceful terraces into a terrace explicitly not generally graceful. These statements establish related machinery, not the candidate's constraints. [Original PDF](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/51/ajc_v51_p243.pdf).

**Ollis (2020), original publisher PDF.** *Sequences in dihedral groups with distinct partial products*, AJC 78(1), 35–60. Example 4.5, p.46, records Walecki. Lemma 5.5, p.53, permits any low start x in a balanced alpha path of length 2p, with end x+p. Theorem 5.6, pp.53–54, inserts a balanced path under an adjacent-pair condition, shifting endpoints; it does not prescribe internal extremes or midpoint. [Original PDF](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/78/ajc_v78_p035.pdf).

Our direct comparison: the candidate core uses each side's offsets `[0,p-1]` with all sums `[0,2p-2]`. Assigning actual low label L and actual high label `2p-1-H` turns it into a balanced alpha path, with differences `[1,2p-1]`. Its endpoints become `2p-2,p-2`; the reverse starts at `p-2` and ends at `(p-2)+p`, exactly consistent with Lemma 5.5. However Lemma 5.5's free endpoint assertion does not locate the edge with the maximum difference in that path. The candidate additionally places its side-zero pair at positions `4s,4s+1`. Its four-entry extension and odd terminal patch also retain that pair while imposing the exceptional high offset set used by the full midpoint path. This is the concrete historical comparison still needed.

The symmetric map from an arbitrary graceful permutation a to `y` followed by reversed `(p-1-y)` is not claimed as new. No accessible original located in this bounded search explicitly identified that exact transform and the residue-3 permutation together. This is an access/search outcome, not a theorem that they are absent from prior literature.

## Remaining access gaps and defensible claim

Original full texts of Rosa, *Labeling snakes* (1977), and Huang–Kotzig–Rosa, *Further results on tree labellings* (1982), were not retrieved. Their relevant statements/attribution are available in Patterson and later primary authors. Cattell full text remains the central gap: its pi-representation constructions may supply the core, doubling, extension or whole compatible path. A complete backwards/forwards citation-chain search and historical finite-table comparison were not performed. Publisher search snippets for McGill–Ollis (2019), *On the asymptotic growth of bipartite graceful permutations*, concern counting paths by starting label and do not close this gap; that article was not relied on for a non-subsumption conclusion.

Recommended private wording after independent mathematical acceptance: “An explicit construction supplies a growing partial set of prescribed zero depths for odd arm lengths, using classical Walecki/path machinery and established alpha amalgamation. Existing results cover ordinary gracefulness and the path/one-center-leaf boundary cases. The exact construction's historical provenance and publication priority remain unresolved.” Do not describe full all-odd zero-rotatability, a new alpha-amalgamation method, or a first proof.
