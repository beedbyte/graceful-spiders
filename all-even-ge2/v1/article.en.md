# Zero-rotatability for spiders with equal even-length arms

Beedbyte

Jordi Gartner owns and publishes this work through Beedbyte.

For every even `k ≥ 2`, `n ≥ 2`, and `m ≥ 0`, each named vertex of `S(k^n,1^m)` can receive zero in a graceful labeling, with a separate labeling for each chosen vertex.

This work proves zero-rotatability for the named spider `S(k^n,1^m)` whenever the long arms have the same even length `k ≥ 2`, there are `n ≥ 2` long arms, and the center has `m ≥ 0` unit leaves. The center, every named position on every long arm, and each existing center leaf can each be assigned zero in a graceful labeling.

Zero-rotatability asks whether the zero label can be placed at any selected vertex while preserving all graceful-labeling conditions. The proof handles lengths two and four directly, then treats the remaining even lengths through balanced midpoint paths, explicit finite certificates, and recursively extended shell constructions. The constructions cover every allowed number of center leaves.

Earlier work supplies relevant overlap and construction tools. Rofa proves zero-rotatability for symmetric spiders with leg length at most three and gives boundary-level results for broader rooted symmetric trees; Luiz, Campos, and Richter cover paths and paths with a central pendant. The four-edge-arm construction uses Krop's generalized subdivision operation and the Huang–Kotzig–Rosa zero/index identification as restated by Shan–Zhong. Hicks–Ollis–Schmitt's three-twizzler graceful permutations supply the length-six path words and graceful-permutation concatenations. Ollis's Lemma 5.5 gives bipartite graceful permutations with endpoint freedom, credited there to Kotzig, and Theorem 5.6 supplies an insertion construction. The complete length-four and length-six cases combine published constructions.
